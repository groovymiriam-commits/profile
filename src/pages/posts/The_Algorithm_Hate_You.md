You post a video. You wait and...

200 - 300 views.

You try again. Another video, same story...

So you do what everyone tells you to do. You follow trends, post every day, and maybe even upload three times a day because some YouTube told you that's the secret sauce.

And still, nothing happens!

At some point, you start thinking the algorithm has a problem with you.

Well, maybe it doesn't. Maybe your video just wasn't that interesting.

I know, not exactly what you wanted to hear.

But here's the thing: just because you think your idea is brilliant doesn't mean anyone else cares about it. And that's probably one of the hardest things to accept when you're creating content.

## Quality Over Quantity

Let's get one thing straight. You don't need to post three times a day to grow on TikTok.

Can posting consistently help? Absolutely. The more you create, the more you learn, and the more opportunities you have to find something that works.

But uploading three average videos a day won't magically turn you into a great creator.

You can post your first or second video and get 50K views. You can even hit over a million. It happens because a video connects with people, not because you spent six months posting every single day.

Instead of asking yourself, "How many videos should I post today?" try asking, "Is this video actually worth watching?"

One good video is worth more than three videos you made just because you felt you had to post something.

And no, spending five hours editing a video doesn't automatically make it good either. You can put a ridiculous amount of effort into something nobody wants to watch. That's the annoying part.

## Your Idea Isn't as Brilliant as You Think

This one hurts a little.

You have an idea. You think it's funny, interesting, clever, or maybe even genius. You're excited about it, so you make a video.

Then it gets 200 views.

Your first instinct is to blame the algorithm. Maybe the timing was wrong. Maybe TikTok didn't push it. Maybe you need better hashtags.

But have you considered that people simply weren't interested?

As creators, we're naturally attached to our own ideas. We know what we meant, why it's funny, and why we think people should care.

Your audience doesn't have that context. They don't know you, they don't owe you their attention, and they're not going to sit through 30 seconds of a video just because you think the ending is brilliant.

**You might love your idea. Your audience needs a reason to love it too.**

This doesn't mean you should give up on an idea just because one video flopped. Sometimes the execution is the problem, not the idea itself.

But you need to be honest enough to tell the difference.

## Start With the Hook

Before you worry about anything else, you need to learn how to get people to stop scrolling.

You can have the most interesting information in the world, but if your opening is boring, most people will never find out.

Instead of starting with a long introduction, get straight to the interesting part. Make people curious. Show them something unexpected. Ask a question they actually want answered.

And please, stop opening every video with "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel." Nobody opened TikTok hoping to attend a meeting.

Once you figure out how to grab attention, you can work on everything else.

Because getting someone to stop scrolling is one thing. Getting them to stay is another.

## Okay, So What About Retention?

You've got a good hook. People stop scrolling. Now what?

Your job is to keep the video interesting enough that they want to continue watching.

Look at your audience retention data in TikTok Analytics. Where are people leaving? Are they dropping off almost immediately, or are they watching most of the video?

If people leave as soon as your introduction ends, that's useful information. If they stay until the interesting part and then leave, that's useful too.

You don't need to guess everything. Your analytics can help you understand what's happening.

You'll sometimes hear that you need a 70% retention rate. But there's no magic number that guarantees success, and retention depends on the video's length and format.

For example, watching three seconds of a five-second video means something very different from watching three seconds of a minute-long video.

Look at the actual data, especially where viewers drop off, rather than chasing a random percentage someone gave you on the internet.

The goal is simple: give people a reason to keep watching.

## Stop Posting Just for the Sake of Posting

You don't need to upload a video every time you open TikTok.

Take the time to develop an idea, figure out how to present it, and make sure there's actually something there worth watching.

And when a video doesn't work, don't immediately assume the idea was terrible or the algorithm hates you.

Maybe the hook wasn't strong enough. Maybe the pacing was slow. Maybe the video made perfect sense to you but wasn't clear to anyone else.

Figure out what you can improve, try a different approach, and move on.

Not every video will perform well. That's part of creating content. Even experienced creators have videos that go nowhere.

But if every video gets 200 views, it's probably worth looking at your content before blaming TikTok.

**The algorithm isn't your content strategist. Stop expecting it to do the work for you.**

Make better videos. Test your ideas. Pay attention to what people actually watch.

And remember: just because you think your video is brilliant doesn't mean your audience agrees.

Now go make something they actually want to watch.
